This isn't "how storage works," the in-game help and the wiki already cover that. It's the question people keep asking anyway: I'm staring at a full bag, what do I actually do with this stuff. The tree and rules below are the fast path, click any question further down for the sourced reasoning behind it. Purple boxes are opinion, a reasonable starting point, not the only right answer; more of my own take is still coming.

Quick rules of thumb

The short version of everything below, for when you don't want to click through the details.

Rule 1

Retainer venture brought back something I don't need: first check if it helps level desynthesis on a class I'm behind on. If not, check whether it sells for over 10k gil with any real frequency on the board. If neither, vendor it or Doman Enclave it right away, don't bank it "just in case."

Rule 2

Under 10 gil on the board, or buyable from any vendor: never worth a bag slot, discard or vendor it immediately.

Rule 3

Old dungeon, trial, or raid gear you've outleveled: if you can easily solo-unsync the fight again, don't hoard it, farming it again costs almost nothing. Only keep it if the content is hard to repeat solo or gone entirely.

Rule 4

Materia: keep the current tier and one tier down, sell everything older, extracting first (not destructive since patch 5.2) if it's still melded to gear you're getting rid of anyway.

Rule 5

Junk gear you were about to vendor: sell it through the Doman Enclave basket instead, it pays a growing multiple of vendor price (up to double), up to the weekly cap.

Rule 6

Quest reward from a finished questline, not on the permanent-loss list: throw it out, the Calamity Salvager has it if you're ever wrong.

Useful lookups

The tree and rules above lean on real numbers for the specific item in your hands, not a guess. These are the sites I actually open to get them.

Universalis

Live cross-world marketboard prices. The gil thresholds in this guide (under 10 gil, over 10k gil) are only useful once you know the actual current number for what you're holding, not a remembered price from months ago.

Garland Tools

Search the item to see its vendor sell price and, for craftable gear, its desynthesis results, what materials and roughly how many you'd actually get. Answers the "desynthesize or just vendor it" question with real data instead of a guess.

FFXIV Collect

If it's a mount, minion, or Triple Triad card, check whether you already own it or it's part of a set you're actually chasing before trading it away as clutter.

Teamcraft

Search the item to see what current recipes, gathering logs, or gearsets actually use it. Useful for the "is this a material I use regularly" question when you're not sure if you'll need it again soon.

Does my gil balance change how much this is worth doing?

Yes, and it's worth saying out loud instead of pretending every player should spend the same effort on this. Purely my own take, no source to cite.

My default
Gil on handHow much effort I'd put in
Under 1 millionRun the full triage on most things. At this range, clearing clutter and using the Doman Enclave meaningfully move your total, it's worth the few extra clicks.
1 million to 10 millionKeep the systems (retainer categories, a Doman Enclave habit, the gil threshold), but stop agonizing over individual items. A wrong call on one stack of materials won't matter.
10 million to 100 millionThe gil value of ordinary clutter is noise against your total. Only declutter for bag-space and sanity, not to optimize gil; don't spend real time hunting for extra value in a pile of mats.
Over 100 millionSkip the analysis entirely. Discard or vendor without thinking, keep only what you'd actually use (glamour, active materia, real collections), and spend money on more retainers or storage instead of hours of triage. Your time is worth more than the marginal gil here.

The free storage most people under-use

Two systems exist specifically to get gear out of your bags for good, and both get ignored for reasons that make sense once you see the friction involved.

What's the Armoire actually for, and why do people ignore it?

It has no slot limit, which sounds amazing, but it only accepts a specific allowlist of gear: job-specific armor sets, seasonal event costumes, achievement rewards, and a handful of bonus items, not just anything. Check for the small lit icon in the item's corner to confirm it qualifies before assuming it belongs there. That restriction, not laziness, is why most players under-use it.

Source: official UI Guide: items storable in the Armoire; FF14 Armoire vs Glamour Dresser.

Armory Chest or Glamour Dresser for my old gear?

Armory Chest is for gear you might actually re-equip: current BiS per role, or gear you're actively using in a rotation. Turn off "store all newly obtained items" so it doesn't fill up with garbage automatically. The Glamour Dresser (800 slots) is for cosmetic keepers only, and it has real friction: you need a Glamour Prism per item, the piece must be at full durability, and materia has to be extracted first. Do that once, deliberately, rather than letting the Armory Chest become a graveyard of half-remembered gear.

Source: Late to the Party Finder: managing your inventory; Basically Average: where to store items.

My default

Check the Armoire first, every time, before doing anything else with a piece of gear; it's free and permanent. If it doesn't qualify, ask whether I'd actually wear it again: yes goes to the Glamour Dresser (batch the prism/durability/materia chores once a month instead of per-item), no goes to Armory Chest only if it's current BiS, otherwise it moves on to the sections below instead of sitting there undecided.

Retainers

How many you actually need, and what to do with them beyond "hold whatever doesn't fit."

PluginAllagan Tools searches every retainer at once instead of opening each one, and Allagan Market tracks listings and undercutting across all of them. See Mods that make this easier.

How many retainers do I actually need, and what should each one do?

You start with 2 free. Up to 7 more are available, but that's a real-money Mogstation subscription (about $2/month per additional slot), not a gil cost, worth knowing before you assume you can just buy more with in-game currency. Each retainer holds 175 slots. One practical split from a veteran with a full 9: three retainers for crafting materials grouped by class, one for finished goods ready to sell, one for materia and collectibles, and the remaining four for glamour and equipment sorted by role. You don't need 9 retainers to benefit from having a system: even 2 or 3 retainers assigned to clear categories beats one retainer holding a little of everything.

Source: veteran reply in SE forum: "Request: Inventory Management Tips from Vets"; retainer counts and costs from TheGamer's retainer guide and the official Retainer Service page.

Can I use an alt character as extra storage?

Yes, and at least one veteran does exactly this: parking level 40+ materials on an alt specifically to keep the main character's bags clear. It's a real, low-effort pattern, not a workaround anyone will judge you for.

Source: SE forum thread #364479.

My default

With only 2-3 retainers, split by function, not by class: one for "things I'm actively selling," one for "everything I'm banking for later." Don't try to mirror the full 9-retainer category split above until you actually have that many; a half-built version of someone else's system is worse than a simple one that fits what you have.

Where things actually go

Main inventory, Armory Chest, and the Chocobo Saddlebag serve different jobs. Using each for what it's built for is most of the battle.

What goes in the Chocobo Saddlebag instead of my main bags?

The saddlebag is usable almost everywhere outside of instanced duties, and it's effectively a separate inventory (it'll even hold a second copy of some unique items your main bags won't). Good candidates: relic weapon materials, dye stacks, tribal and event tokens and other currencies, and gardening supplies if you garden seriously. You get 70 slots free, or 140 with the Companion App subscription.

Source: Basically Average; Late to the Party Finder.

I'm leveling multiple jobs or multiple crafters. How do I keep the Armory Chest from running out of space?

Weapons are the real bottleneck. Every job and class has its own unique weapon, they're never shared between jobs, and there's no way around needing one Main Hand slot per job you keep geared. As of Dawntrail that's 21 standard combat jobs plus the limited job Blue Mage, plus 8 crafters and 3 gatherers, roughly 33 weapons for a true completionist. Square Enix knows this is tight: patch 6.0 bumped the Main Hand and Rings tabs specifically from 35 to 50 slots (and removed the Waist tab entirely, since belts were cut from the game the same patch). Every other tab, Head, Body, Hands, Legs, Feet, Earrings, Necklace, Bracelets, is still 35, and there's no Mogstation item, achievement, or quest that lets you buy or unlock more, unlike retainers.

Crafters and gatherers have it much easier than combat jobs on the armor side: current-tier crafting/gathering armor (body, hands, legs, feet, most accessories) is generally usable by all 8 Disciples of the Hand at once, not job-locked, so you're mostly only paying for one weapon per crafting job, not a full gear set. The exception is class-specific Artifact Armor at level milestones 50/60/70/80, which is job-locked like combat gear. Rings, earrings, necklaces, and bracelets are stat-based rather than job-locked too, so a single accessory often covers every job sharing that stat spread, which is exactly why the Rings tab got the same capacity bump as weapons in 6.0.

Two concrete tricks from long-time multi-job players: don't keep a second physical copy of a weapon in Armory purely for glamour, right-click it into the Glamour Dresser and apply it manually per job instead, that's a free slot back per job you're not using a matching plate for. And when a gearset says you don't have room for a ring even though the Rings tab isn't full, it's usually because different gearsets have the same ring type sitting in different gearset slots, put the same ring type in the same slot across every job's gearset and the false "not enough space" error goes away.

Source: official patch 6.0 notes; SE forum "Armory Space"; Basically Average.

My default
  • Main inventory: only what I'd need mid-duty, food, potions, current job's gear, active quest items.
  • Armory Chest: current BiS per role I actually play, nothing benched "just in case."
  • Saddlebag: currencies, dyes, and relic/event materials I'm actively accumulating toward something.
  • Retainer/alt: everything else worth keeping at all.
  • Jobs I'm not actively playing: bank the weapon on a retainer or alt instead of leaving it in Armory. Crafting/gathering tools especially, most are cheap to buy or craft again, so an old one isn't worth a permanent slot just in case.

Materials, materia & small collectibles

The stuff that quietly eats the most slots, and a rule of thumb for clearing it out without regret.

PluginRepairMe reminds you when a piece of gear still has materia to extract, so you never accidentally desynthesize or sell it away for free. See Mods that make this easier.

My crafting mat tab is a disaster. What's safe to vendor or discard?

If a material is buyable from any NPC vendor, or it's not clearing more than about 10 gil on the marketboard, it's not earning its slot: toss it or vendor it. Downgrade stray HQ stacks to NQ if you're not actively using the HQ bonus. If your gatherers are high-level, you can regather most raw materials on demand anyway, so there's little reason to bank them "just in case."

Source: veteran replies in SE forum thread #364479; corroborated by Late to the Party Finder.

What do meld, spiritbond, and overmeld actually mean?

Melding is attaching a materia to an empty materia slot on a piece of gear for a stat bonus. A Materia Melder NPC (found near most market boards) will do it for a small gil fee, 50 gil for Grade I materia up to 2,000 gil for Grade XII, once you've completed the quest "Forging the Spirit" (Swynbroes, The Bonfire, Central Thanalan). Completing a separate quest, "Waking the Spirit" (level 19+, with a Disciple of the Hand at or above your gear's level), lets you meld it yourself instead of paying an NPC.

Overmelding is adding materia beyond a piece of gear's normal slots, via "advanced melding," which you have to do yourself, NPCs can't do it. It has a real chance to fail and destroy the materia you're trying to meld: roughly 17% success on the first overmeld slot, dropping to 10%, 7%, and 5% on the slots after that with current endgame materia. That's why overmeld fodder is specifically valuable, you'll burn through several materia per successful overmeld.

Spiritbond builds up on a piece of gear as you use it in battle, crafting, or gathering, shown by the gear's status dots turning from green to white at 100%. Since patch 5.2, reaching 100% and extracting materia is non-destructive: you keep the item and get the materia both, this used to destroy the item pre-5.2 and was called "conversion," which is why older guides and forum posts still say "convert." If you're getting rid of a piece of gear anyway, spiritbond it to 100% and extract first, then desynthesize or sell the (now materia-free) item afterward, there's no reason to skip the free materia.

Source: Materia, Console Games Wiki.

Which materia is safe to sell or discard?

Keep the current top grade (Grade VIII as of this writing) and the grade just below it, since that's usually the best fodder for overmelding. Grade VI is worth holding onto while leveling specifically because it has a 100% retrieval chance when you re-extract it. Anything older that you've already outleveled the source gear for is safe to clear out.

Source: veteran replies in SE forum thread #364479; Thonky's materia guide; Materia, Console Games Wiki.

I've got a pile of Triple Triad cards, fireworks, and orchestrion rolls. Worth keeping?

Triple Triad cards have no marketboard value at all, they can only be sold to an NPC for MGP, so if you're not actively collecting the set, trade them in rather than let them sit. Unused fireworks and duplicate orchestrion rolls (a roll you already own doesn't stack usefully) are safe clutter to clear.

Source: Late to the Party Finder; Triple Triad marketboard status cross-checked in this site's own trade-in guide research.

My default

Gil threshold for materials: under 10 gil on the board or vendor-buyable, it goes, no exceptions. Materia: keep current grade plus one grade down, everything else gets sold, or spiritbond-extracted first if it's still attached to gear I'm getting rid of anyway. Collectibles I'm not actively completing a set for (cards, fireworks, duplicate rolls) get liquidated the same week I notice them, not "eventually."

Other things to do with clutter besides the trash

Before you discard or plain-vendor something, two systems can turn it into more value than either option: desynthesis and the Doman Enclave.

Is desynthesis actually worth doing, and do I need a high skill level first?

No level requirement to worry about: as of patch 5.2, your desynthesis skill level no longer affects your success chance at all, that was a common misconception in older guides. What skill level actually changes is reward quality: desynthesizing gear well above your current skill gives a wider variety of materials, larger quantities, or a better chance at HQ materials. Eight crafting classes can desynthesize (Carpenter, Blacksmith, Armorer, Goldsmith, Leatherworker, Weaver, Alchemist, Culinarian), each with its own independent skill level, unlocked at level 30 via the quest "Gone to Pieces." It works on most gear, housing items, and even fish, and turns something you'd otherwise vendor for a pittance into real crafting materials instead. It's permanent, so don't desynthesize anything you might still want.

Source: Desynthesis, Console Games Wiki.

Is there a better place to sell junk gear than a regular vendor? (New player primer: the Doman Enclave)

Yes, and if you've never heard of it, this is worth knowing early. The Doman Enclave is a district in Kugane with its own Shazenkai, and inside it a Donation Basket that acts like a vendor who pays you more than a vendor. Every item you donate there sells for a multiple of its normal vendor price, not a flat bonus, a multiple: it starts at 120% of vendor price, and climbs in stages the more you personally donate over time, to 150% (after roughly 40,000 gil donated), 180% (roughly 85,000 gil), and finally 200%, double the normal vendor payout, once you've donated roughly 160,000 gil worth of items total.

There's a weekly limit on how much of that bonus gil the basket will pay out, though: it starts at 20,000 gil per week and rises in the same stages up to 40,000 gil per week at the higher milestones. It resets every Tuesday at 8am GMT. Once you hit the weekly cap, extra donations that week just pay normal vendor price until it resets, so it's worth spreading big sell-offs across weeks once you're relying on it.

To unlock it at all, you need to have cleared a short chain of Stormblood MSQ-adjacent quests ("Elation and Trepidation," "Short Arms of the Law," and "A Thousand and One Farewells"), then started "Precious Reclamation," which begins the reconstruction questline and opens the basket. The reconstruction storyline itself concluded years ago, but the basket keeps working exactly the same way today, it just doesn't hand out new one-time storyline rewards anymore. If you donate something by mistake, any of your last 100 donated items can be reclaimed for a small fee.

Source: Doman Enclave Reconstruction, Console Games Wiki; Doman Reconstruction, Gamer Escape.

My default

For anything I've decided to get rid of that isn't safe to just discard: if it's craftable gear or a weapon I don't want, desynthesize it instead of vendoring, the materials are worth more than the gil most of the time. For everything else I'd otherwise plain-vendor, sell it through the Doman Enclave basket up to the weekly cap first, then fall back to a normal vendor once the cap's hit.

Quest items & currencies

Most quest clutter is safer to clear than it looks, thanks to one specific NPC.

Can I just discard my quest reward items once I'm done with that questline?

Almost always, yes. Most quest-related items you discard can be repurchased later from the Calamity Salvager in Limsa Lominsa, Gridania, or Ul'dah, that NPC exists specifically so new players don't ruin their game by clearing bag space. The exceptions are rare enough to list explicitly (see below), so don't let one edge case make you a hoarder of everything.

Source: Calamity Salvager, Console Games Wiki; SE forum: items that can never be reacquired.

What about all the tomestones, scrips, and event currencies piling up?

Short answer here, the longer answer deserves its own page: several currencies have a hard cap or rotate out entirely on a patch cycle, silently converting or vanishing if you don't spend them down first. This is common enough that it's its own guide idea on this site rather than something to fully solve in this FAQ.

Source: TheGamer: the tomestone cycle-exchange explained.

What never comes back

A short list on purpose. Everything else quest-related is almost certainly safe, and is on the Calamity Salvager's buy-back list if you're not sure.

Confirmed permanent once discarded:

  • The starting Ring of Freedom, Fortune, Fidelity, or Fortitude (whichever you picked at character creation)
  • PvP ranked-season rewards (no Armory Chest safety net for those)
  • A handful of 1.x-era relics, like the original Onion Helmet and the Dalamud Horn
  • Gear tied to removed content, such as the old Diadem's i280 Aetherial weapons

Source: SE forum: "Items that can never be acquired again after they are discarded".

What not to do

Habits that quietly undo everything above. None of these are about a single item, they're patterns worth noticing in yourself.

Speculative hoarding. Keeping materials or gear "in case the price goes up later" with no target price, no check-in date, and no plan. That's not market timing, it's just storage. If you're not actually tracking it on Universalis, sell it the same week you get it.

Half-building someone else's system. Copying the full 9-retainer category split from this guide when you only have 2 or 3 retainers leaves you with a worse mess than just picking a simple split that fits what you actually have.

Leaving "store all newly obtained items" switched on. That one setting is what turns the Armory Chest into a graveyard of everything you've ever picked up instead of gear you actually chose to keep.

Panic-discarding without checking the permanent-loss list. The flip side of hoarding: clearing bag space so aggressively you toss something that's actually gone for good. When unsure, check the list above or the Calamity Salvager's buy-back before you decide.

Melding or overmelding gear you're not sure you're keeping. Overmeld fodder already has a low success rate, don't burn it into a piece you might replace next patch. Confirm it's a keeper first.

Letting currencies sit until they cap or expire. The "I'll deal with it later" mistake with weekly caps and patch-cycle tomestones. You don't lose them all at once, you just quietly stop earning more until you spend down.
